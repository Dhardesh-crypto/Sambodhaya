# วัดสัมโพธายะ · Sambodhaya Online Temple (prototype)

Child-facing (10+) prototype of the Sambodhaya Online Temple, in the visual style of Dhamma Land.
**Not authorised for public release** (Library: "Public release: NOT PASSED / NOT AUTHORIZED"). The page carries `noindex` and a visible prototype banner. Do not deploy publicly until the release gate passes.

## What's in it
- **Entrance:** the illustrated temple (`img/entrance.jpg`, used unchanged) as a full-width hero with clickable areas for each hall, the gates and the temple dog. On phones the picture is a banner and the hubs become large cards.
- **Hubs:** Central Courtyard, Learning Hall (U01–U08 stories in the five-minute pattern), Evidence Library, Practice Garden (body • feelings • mind • Dhamma), My Journey (U01–U08 path), Media Hall (includes the wheel diagram), Universal Hall (language and access settings), Rest Area, Caretaker, Exit.
- Practice Garden and My Journey sit inside the existing Learning–Practice–Observation hall; they are entrances, not new halls.

## 3D temple (`world.html`, trial)
A solo, Roblox-style low-poly walk through the same temple, built with Three.js (loaded from jsDelivr; no build step). Halls, the great tree, ponds, the Practice Garden's four stones and the eight My Journey stepping stones follow the entrance artwork. Walking up to a place and pressing E (or tapping) opens a panel that links to the matching page of the 2D site, so the 3D world never holds teaching content of its own.

**The way into 3D from the entrance page:** the entrance offers two equal ways in under the hero picture, "🚪 Enter the temple" (the pages) and "🏯 Walk the temple in 3D", each with a picture (the 3D one is a snapshot of the 3D temple, `img/world-preview.jpg`). On larger screens a "🏯 Walk the temple in 3D" button also sits on the hero picture, centred under the great tree, so it is visible without scrolling; on phones the two ways in are compact enough to fit on the first screen. In text-only mode they show icons instead of pictures.

**Showcase upgrade (Oct 2026):** sunset-sky lighting, real materials, soft shadows and a gentle glow, plus a walk-in **Learning Hall** (open Thai sala) with eight U01–U08 story boards along its side walls that open the matching lesson. A ✨/⚡ button switches between detailed and light graphics (light is picked automatically on weaker phones and remembered on that device only).

**Every hall is now a walk-in sala (Oct 2026):** the Evidence Library (manuscript shelves, reading tables), the Media Hall (the Sambodhaya wheel image, unchanged, on the back wall, with benches), the Universal Hall, the Caretaker's kuti (with the temple dog's bed) and an open Rest pavilion by the pond. Also new: a Practice Garden with a rock cliff, boulders and bamboo; a white temple wall with lotus-bud posts; Thai trees (frangipani, sugar palm, banana) and bougainvillea. A moat surrounds the temple wall; visitors start beyond it, cross an arched bridge (you walk over its arch), enter through a gateway in the wall, and follow one continuous path to the Bodhi tree.

**Learning Hall and Universal Hall seating follow temple custom:** visitors and students sit on the floor; teachers and monks sit on a raised platform along the back wall (not walkable for visitors); the Buddha image sits on a stepped altar on that platform, always the highest, with candles, phan phum offerings and flower vases.

**Kathina (ทอดกฐิน), offering the robe to the Sangha:** in the Learning Hall, the temple's main hall where the senior monk sits, a low table on the platform to his left holds the Kathina gifts (folded robes, an alms bowl, a folded umbrella, a candle and a pillow), in place of the empty seat there; the front row of floor cushions leaves room before him to kneel and bow. Standing before the senior monk (the elder, left of the altar) and pressing E (🟫 ถวายผ้ากฐิน), the boy kneels close to the platform holding the folded robe on a gold pedestal tray in both hands, raises it to his forehead and sets it on the edge of the platform before the monk, who leans in and draws it toward himself: he receives it for the whole community of monks. The boy brings his hands to a wai at the chest, moves back on his knees, and then pays respect to the monk exactly as to the Buddha: the high wai and three bows with forehead and palms on the floor, then he stands. The robe stays before the monk. No chant text is shown (the charter keeps teachings verbatim). In Thailand Kathina is held once a year, in the month after the end of the Rains Retreat; here it can be made at any time.

**Shoes off at every hall:** as at a Thai temple, shoes stay outside. Walking up to any hall (or the open Rest pavilion), the boy stops on the step, looks down and slips out of his sneakers one foot at a time; the pair is left neatly on the step beside him, toes turned out, with his school backpack standing beside it (sitting and bowing with a bag on isn't done either), and he goes in in his white socks. Coming out, he steps back into his shoes and takes his bag. Inside he is a second model of the same boy, in socks and without the backpack (made from the same artwork, with the same skeleton and the same idle, walk, run and sitting animations), so the wai, sitting, the kraap and everything posed on his skeleton work the same; the measurements those poses rely on (his hands, his fingers' direction, his forehead) are taken from whichever model he is wearing. Arriving inside some other way (the places menu, coming back from a page) he is simply in socks with his shoes at the step. Only the Sambodhaya boy changes; the built characters have no shoes to take off. The socks model loads after the boy appears, so the first steps are never delayed.

**Giving alms (ตักบาตร):** four monks stand in a row on the entrance road just outside the temple gate, most senior first (the elder, the middle-aged monk, the young monk with glasses and a novice), each holding his black alms bowl open. On an alms round the robe covers both shoulders (ห่มคลุม), so these monks are dressed that way. A low table at the roadside holds a silver bowl of sticky-rice balls. Standing in front of a monk and pressing E (🍚 ใส่บาตร), the boy steps up and faces him, holds a rice ball in both hands, raises it to his forehead, places it in the bowl (his eyes on the bowl, the monk leaning in slightly to receive), lowers his hands and gives the high wai. His hips and shoulders turn square to the monk for the offering, and the rice is held between his palms (placed by measurement of his hands). The rice stays in the bowl; nothing is counted. In Thailand givers usually take off their shoes; the boy's shoes are part of his model, so he keeps them on.

**Monks on the platform:** three different monks (an elder, a middle-aged monk and a young monk with glasses), in the same animated-film style as the schoolboy, sit cross-legged on the teachers' seats in the Learning Hall, the temple's main hall (the Universal Hall's seats stand empty). Their robes are the muted brown of the forest tradition (not bright orange), worn temple-style with the right shoulder bare; they always sit lower than the Buddha image. They breathe slowly (still when calm motion is on) and are scenery only: they do not speak or teach, so the 3D world still holds no teaching content of its own.

**The Sambodhaya boy (default avatar):** the boy from the Sambodhaya film as a rigged 3D character (Higgsfield: a clean picture made from the film's frames, then Meshy image-to-3D with a 24-bone skeleton). He has idle, casual-walk and run animations (`boy.glb` holds the model and walk; `boy-idle.glb` and `boy-run.glb` hold only an animation for the same skeleton). Each clip's stride is measured when it loads and the clip plays at the speed that keeps his feet planted; footstep sounds follow his steps. Normal movement is a calm walk, Shift (or the touch stick pushed to its edge) runs. The block builder, picture-face and 3D-file avatars remain under "My avatar". **The wai (🙏 button, Q, or the 🙏 touch button):** palms pressed flat together in front of the chest, fingers together and upright, fingertips meeting at the chin, head facing forward with a modest bow; it holds briefly and rises again. The height follows Thai custom and where he stands: in front of a Buddha image (the Learning Hall and Universal Hall shrines) he turns to face it and makes the highest wai - thumbs between the eyebrows, fingertips at the hairline, a deeper bow and a longer hold; in the caretaker's kuti, the wai to elders - fingertips between the eyebrows; elsewhere, the everyday wai - fingertips at the nose. The heights are points on his own face, measured from his head mesh, and the hands follow the face as he bows. The hands are placed from the boy's real hand mesh (the palm side is the hand's thinnest direction that faces the body at rest), so the fingertips meet exactly; where the thick cartoon palms would pass the middle they are pressed flat against each other in the shader, as real palms flatten, so the hands never pass through each other. No ready-made wai animation exists, so it is posed on the boy's own skeleton (a two-bone reach solver places the arms; the bow is spread over the spine, neck and head) on top of whatever he is doing; walking away ends it gently. The block avatar does a simpler block-style wai.

**Sitting (🧘 button, C, or the 🧘 touch button):** the boy lowers himself into a cross-legged sit (ขัดสมาธิ, a ready-made floor-sitting animation) with his eyes lowered and his hands resting in his lap, palms up, the right hand on the left (posed on his skeleton; the lap height is measured from his seated legs, and the two thick hands are pressed flat where they meet). Near a cushion in a hall he settles onto it, facing the front - the altar and the teachers' platform - to meditate or listen; before a Buddha image he faces the image. A wai while seated works (in a shrine hall, the high wai). The temple dog comes and sits beside him (its body tips back over the front paws so the haunches rest on the ground, in its vertex shader) and gets up when he does. Walking, or the button again, stands him up. The block avatar sits with legs forward and hands on its knees.

**Paying tribute to the Buddha (🙇 กราบพระ button or K):** in front of the Buddha image in the Learning Hall or the Universal Hall, the boy walks to the aisle before the altar (far enough back that his bowed head stays clear of the platform), faces the image and kneels (toes tucked, as boys kneel) with his hands in a wai at the chest. He raises a high wai (fingertips at the hairline), then bows three times (กราบ 3 ครั้ง): forehead to the floor with both palms flat on the floor beside it, then back up to kneeling with the high wai each time. After the third bow he lowers the wai to his chest and stands. It takes about 14 seconds and is posed on his skeleton (the forehead and palms are placed on the floor by measurement, the knees stay on the floor). The temple dog sits quietly while he bows. Elsewhere, the button explains where it can be done.

**Movement:** the temple dog trots (diagonal legs swing together, paws lift, head bobs) and wags its tail; the model has no skeleton, so the walk is done in its vertex shader, shadow included. The Practice Garden waterfall runs: streaks flow down a curved sheet of water in two layers, foam turns where it lands, ripples spread across the pool, droplets splash and a soft mist hangs. Calm motion slows the water and the tail rather than stopping them.

**Donations in 3D (for adults):** a red-and-gold donation box (ตู้รับบริจาค) on the front of the inner gate's "leave any time" post; standing at it and pressing E opens a short note (for adults, entirely optional, the temple is the same for everyone) and a link to the support page. Quiet "(for adults)" links also sit in the ❔ help and 🗺️ places panels. All of them carry the way back to the 3D temple.

**Between the 3D temple and the site:** pages opened from inside the 3D temple (a hall, a story board, 💬 feedback) carry `?from=3d`; there the site's "back to courtyard" becomes "↩️🏯 back to the 3D temple", and you return to the exact spot, facing and view you left (kept for this browser tab only). The browser's back button returns there too. Pages opened directly on the site, and the 3D temple's "leave" and "no 3D map" links, behave as before.

**Sound (`src/sound.js`):** made in the browser with Web Audio, nothing is downloaded. Following the charter, nothing plays until the visitor presses something: the 🔈 button (off every time the page opens) starts the ambience, and the temple bell rings only when struck (walk to it, press E). The site-wide "turn off all sound" setting is respected: the button and the bell ask first. Sounds are placed in 3D:
- breeze and rustling leaves that follow slow gusts, and small roof bells (kradueng) at the hall eaves that tinkle in the gusts
- birds in the trees: bulbul-like twitters and, now and then, the Asian koel's rising "ko-el"
- the Practice Garden waterfall
- footsteps: stone on paths and the plaza, soft on grass, hollow wood inside halls
- inside a hall the outside world is muffled and quieter
- the bronze temple bell: a deep strike with a long, beating ring

Asset credits:
- `assets/tex/`: textures (paving, roof tiles, plaster, wood, grass, mossy rock) and the `belfast_sunset_puresky` sky from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com) (CC0).
- `assets/models/`: the Bodhi tree, frangipani trees, lamp posts, lanterns, bell, lotus bowls, jars, altar candles, reading table, Buddha image, phan phum, flower vases, bookshelves, benches, plants, rocks and the temple dog, the seated monks and the alms-round monks, the boy in his socks, his sneakers and backpack on the step were generated for this project with Higgsfield (Tripo 3D). The dog was made from the temple dog in the entrance artwork. All models were simplified and their textures converted to WebP with gltf-transform; every model falls back to simple shapes if it can't load.
- Avatars: build a blocky character (saved on the device), use a picture as the face (kept only if the player ticks "remember"; drawings recommended for children), or upload a `.glb`/`.vrm` model (never stored; VRMs get a relaxed pose and a procedural walk, glTF clips named idle/walk are used when present, and a turn-around button fixes models that face backwards).
- The temple dog follows the player and opens a "where would you like to go?" list (scripted, not an AI). A plain places list, a calm-motion toggle and a link back to the 2D map are always available. No points, collectibles, streaks or automatic sound.
- Solo only: nothing a player does leaves the device. Multiplayer would need a real-time server and its own child-safety design.

## Where the content comes from
All teaching text is copied verbatim from the Sambodhaya Notion pages; nothing is AI-authored teaching:
- `APP CONTENT v1 — U01–U08 Child Output Records`: story text, child questions, life line, sources, boundaries, uncertainty, safety, status
- `U01–U08 EXERCISE SET v1 — Publication Draft`: exercises (shown as draft, no score)
- `ภาษาหน้าบ้านเด็ก 10+`, `ENTRANCE`, `CENTRAL COURTYARD`, `LEARN • PRACTICE • OBSERVE`, `QUIET REST AREA`, `TEMPLE CARETAKER`, `หอพระไตรปิฎก`: zone wording

Interface wording (buttons, notices) was written for this prototype. English is an unreviewed AI draft and is labelled as such.
`img/sambodhaya-wheel.png` (wheel diagram) and `img/entrance.jpg` (entrance artwork) are the project's own images, used unchanged; `img/temple-dog.jpg` is cropped from the entrance artwork. `img/temple-bg.webp` comes from Dhamma Land. The wheel is shown in the Media Hall as explanatory media, status under review.
