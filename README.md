# วัดสัมโพธายะ · Sambodhaya Online Temple (prototype)

Child-facing (10+) prototype of the Sambodhaya Online Temple, in the visual style of Dhamma Land.
**Not authorised for public release** (Library: "Public release: NOT PASSED / NOT AUTHORIZED"). The page carries `noindex` and a visible prototype banner. Do not deploy publicly until the release gate passes.

## What's in it
- **Entrance:** the illustrated temple (`img/entrance.jpg`, used unchanged) as a full-width hero with clickable areas for each hall, the gates and the temple dog. On phones the picture is a banner and the hubs become large cards.
- **Hubs:** Central Courtyard, Learning Hall (U01–U08 stories in the five-minute pattern), Evidence Library, Practice Garden (body • feelings • mind • Dhamma), My Journey (U01–U08 path), Media Hall (includes the wheel diagram), Universal Hall (language and access settings), Rest Area, Caretaker, Exit.
- Practice Garden and My Journey sit inside the existing Learning–Practice–Observation hall; they are entrances, not new halls.
- **Supporting the temple (🤲, for adults, `#support`):** two steps. First only the transfer details: the PromptPay QR code and the bank account side by side (stacked on phones; until the team provides the QR, a marked placeholder stands in its place), the recipient and the tax note, with a button "I've completed the transfer – attach the slip"; then the slip form: name (or anonymous), amount, the slip, an optional message and email, how to be thanked, and one consent line ("I am 18+ and consent to my slip data being kept for audit purposes (90 days)"), with what the data is used for and where to ask for deletion written below it. There are no date or time fields: the slip shows both. What contributions go towards folds out below the button. (Suggested by Arnon, 13 Oct 2026.)
- **Listening (🎧):** each story's short version (U01–U08, Thai and English) is recorded in one calm teacher's voice, the same on every device (`audio/u01-th.mp3` … `audio/u08-en.mp3`; an AI voice, "Mark", made with MiniMax through Higgsfield, from the exact text in `src/content.js`; 64 kbps mono). The 🎧 button plays it and ⏸️ stops it; it is greyed out when "turn off all sound" is on. If a recording can't play, the device's own voice reads the text instead. A 🕯️ note says it is an AI voice, not yet a reviewed narration. If a story's text changes, its recording must be made again.

## 3D temple (`world.html`, trial)
A solo, Roblox-style low-poly walk through the same temple, built with Three.js (loaded from jsDelivr; no build step). Halls, the great tree, ponds, the Practice Garden's four stones and the eight My Journey stepping stones follow the entrance artwork. Walking up to a place and pressing E (or tapping) opens a panel that links to the matching page of the 2D site, so the 3D world never holds teaching content of its own.

**The way into 3D from the entrance page:** the entrance offers two equal ways in under the hero picture, "🚪 Enter the temple" (the pages) and "🏯 Walk the temple in 3D", each with a picture (the 3D one is a snapshot of the 3D temple, `img/world-preview.jpg`). On larger screens a "🏯 Walk the temple in 3D" button also sits on the hero picture, centred under the great tree, so it is visible without scrolling; on phones the two ways in are compact enough to fit on the first screen. In text-only mode they show icons instead of pictures.

**Showcase upgrade (Oct 2026):** sunset-sky lighting, real materials, soft shadows and a gentle glow, plus a walk-in **Learning Hall** (open Thai sala) with eight U01–U08 story boards along its side walls that open the matching lesson. A "✨ Graphics: detailed" / "⚡ Graphics: light" button switches between detailed and light graphics (light leaves out the garden, shadows and glow) (light is picked automatically on weaker phones and remembered on that device only).

**Every hall is now a walk-in sala (Oct 2026):** the Evidence Library (manuscript shelves, reading tables), the Media Hall (the Sambodhaya wheel image, unchanged, on the back wall, with benches), the Universal Hall, the Caretaker's kuti (with the temple dog's bed) and an open Rest pavilion by the pond. Also new: a Practice Garden with a rock cliff, boulders and bamboo; a white temple wall with lotus-bud posts; Thai trees (frangipani, sugar palm, banana) and bougainvillea. A moat surrounds the temple wall; visitors start beyond it, cross an arched bridge (you walk over its arch), enter through a gateway in the wall, and follow one continuous path to the Bodhi tree.

**A Thai temple garden:** the grounds are planted the way Thai temples are: big shade rain trees and golden shower trees (ราชพฤกษ์) between the halls, red ixora (เข็ม) and heliconia lining every path and bordering the plaza and the sides of each hall, khao mo rockeries (เขามอ) at the edges of the ponds, groups of limestone rocks with ferns, and a pair of bonsai (ไม้ดัด) in glazed pots either side of each hall's steps. Nothing is placed by hand: every spot is checked against the paths, halls, steps, ponds, plaza, Practice Garden, My Journey stones, the alms row and everything already standing, and the trees stand well back from the paths so the camera doesn't swing into their leaves. The same garden grows every time (a fixed seed). It is decorative only, so it is left out in the light quality mode used on smaller phones.

**Learning Hall and Universal Hall seating follow temple custom:** visitors and students sit on the floor; teachers and monks sit on a raised platform along the back wall (not walkable for visitors); the Buddha image sits on a stepped altar on that platform, always the highest, with candles, phan phum offerings and flower vases.

**Kathina (ทอดกฐิน), offering the robe to the Sangha:** in the Learning Hall, the temple's main hall where the senior monk sits, a low table on the platform to his left holds the Kathina gifts (folded robes, an alms bowl, a folded umbrella, a candle and a pillow), in place of the empty seat there; the front row of floor cushions leaves room before him to kneel and bow. Before the senior monk (the elder, left of the altar), pressing E and choosing 🟫 ถวายผ้ากฐิน (Offer the Kathina robe), the boy kneels close to the platform holding the folded robe on a gold pedestal tray in both hands, raises it to his forehead and sets it on the platform just in front of the monk's cushion, and the monk lays both hands on the tray: he receives it for the whole community of monks. The boy brings his hands to a wai at the chest, moves back on his knees, and then pays respect to the monk exactly as to the Buddha: the high wai and three bows with forehead and palms on the floor. Still kneeling, with the wai at his chest and his head a little bowed, he is blessed with holy water (ประพรมน้ำมนต์): the monk takes the straw whisk (ไม้พรม) from the bowl of holy water at his right knee and, while he recites the blessing (below), dips it and flicks drops over the child three times, with a soft patter of water, then lays it back across the bowl; then the boy stands. The robe stays before the monk. All three teachers in this hall can move: the head monk, หลวงพ่อเปลี่ยน (Luang Por Plien); the monk of about forty, พระอาจารย์พิทยา (Phra Ajahn Pittaya); and the young monk with glasses, พระวิโมกข์ (Phra Vimoke). Their names show when the child kneels before them, and in the passport. Each is a model sculpted already seated cross-legged, so the robe lies smoothly over the folded legs, and each has a skeleton for the upper body (back, neck, head and arms) built when the model loads, from joints measured on that model. The legs and the robe over the lap are never deformed. At rest each sits like a meditating monk: back straight, eyes lowered, hands resting in the lap with the palms up and the right hand on the left. Each breathes slowly, now and then turns his head a little, and looks at the child when the child is close in front of him. The senior monk's hands leave the lap to receive the robe and to bless, then return. More actions can be added to all three later. No chant text is shown (the charter keeps teachings verbatim). In Thailand Kathina is held once a year, in the month after the end of the Rains Retreat; here it can be made at any time.

**Making merit (ทำบุญ):** three of the everyday offerings at a Thai temple, each started by standing at the spot and pressing E (ทำบุญ):
- **Incense, candles and a lotus (ธูปเทียนดอกไม้):** in both shrine halls a low incense table stands on the floor against the middle of the platform, below the Buddha image, with two candles, a bronze incense urn of sand and a vase. As at a Thai temple, it is all done kneeling: the boy kneels before the table, lights the two candles, lights three incense sticks from them, holds the sticks and a lotus bud between his palms in the high wai, plants the incense in the urn and the lotus in the vase, brings his hands to a wai at the chest, moves back on his knees and bows three times (the kraap, forehead and palms to the floor, clear of the table). The candles and incense burn on for a few minutes, smoke rising.
- **Gold leaf (ปิดทอง):** in the Universal Hall a small bronze Buddha image stands on a low stand before the platform. Kneeling before it, the boy makes the high wai, presses a leaf of gold onto the image, makes the high wai again, moves back on his knees and bows three times. The gold stays where he pressed it, building up over the visit.
- **Cloth on the Bodhi tree (ผ้าสามสี):** at the edge of the great tree's platform he makes a wai holding a folded three-coloured cloth, and a new band of cloth appears around the trunk, shaped to it.
- **Sweeping the courtyard (กวาดลานวัด):** a broom leans on its stand beside the plaza. Pressing E there picks it up (E again puts it back); walking with it sweeps away the fallen leaves it passes over, and new leaves keep drifting down from the trees. He leans it back by himself before any other action.

Nothing is counted or scored, and the dog sits while he makes an offering. (The kneeling offerings and the bows reuse the kraap's posing; the kraap before an image is now made a little further back, clear of the incense table.)

**A living temple:** the temple goes on with its day around the visitor. None of them speaks or teaches; they are scenery.
- **Walking meditation (เดินจงกรม):** a monk walks slowly up and down a lane of raked sand on the lawn by the kuti, hands clasped before him (the right holding the left), eyes lowered to the ground ahead. He slows as he nears each end, stands a moment with his feet together and turns round slowly in small steps. If someone stands in his way, he waits.
- **Watering the plants (รดน้ำต้นไม้):** a green watering can stands by a water jar across the entrance road from the broom. Pressing E there picks it up (E again puts it back); the child carries it at the side. At a potted plant (the two by the gate, and the bonsai either side of every hall's steps: 14 in all) E waters it: he squares up to the plant, brings the can forward and tips it, and a fine stream with a few drops falls on the soil, with a soft patter. Each plant once a visit; a small note counts how many are done. Sweeping 12 leaves and watering a plant each earn a stamp in the passport (Helping).
- **Folding a lotus (พับกลีบบัว):** in the Universal Hall a woven mat lies on the floor, with a brass tray of lotus buds. Pressing E there, the child sits on the mat with a bud in the lap, and a panel shows the lotus from the front: each petal of the outer row is tapped to fold it the Thai way, in half lengthwise with the tip tucked in, then the second row; the bud in the middle stays closed (the way a former monk in Thailand described it). The lotus in his hands folds as he goes. The folded lotus goes with the next offering of incense, candles and a lotus at a shrine: into the vase, kneeling, with three bows. It earns a stamp in the passport (Helping).
- **A novice sweeping:** a young novice sweeps his way round the courtyard about the Bodhi tree and back with a long broom, stopping now and then to sweep one spot; the fallen leaves before his broom are brushed away. He waits for anyone in his way. At night he has gone in.
- **The temple cat:** a Siamese temple cat (วิเชียรมาศ) sleeps curled up on the end of the Evidence Library's step, breathing slowly. Now and then she wakes, stretches (front legs far forward, hindquarters up, a yawn), and wanders the lawn before the library, sitting a while at each of a few places, then goes back to her step and curls up again (at night she stays asleep). She waits for anyone in her way. Walk up to her and press E (🐈 ลูบแมว) to pet her: the boy kneels and strokes her (leaning in if she is asleep); she sits for it and purrs (with the sound on); a sleeping cat wakes and stretches afterwards. She is four models of the same cat, one per pose, changing over with a short fade; her walk and her swaying tail are done on the graphics card, like the dog's.
- **Pigeons:** a few pigeons walk and peck on the plaza, nodding as they go. When the boy comes close (sooner if he runs) or the dog does, they clatter up and fly into the trees, and come back down a while later somewhere quieter. At night they roost.
The monk and the novice are rigged like the boy (the same skeleton, with their own walking animation), so their hands are posed the same way; standing, they rest in the walk's feet-together moment, and the monk turns at the end of his lane in small slow steps, in one smooth movement; they load after the boy, so his first steps are never delayed.

**Petting the dog:** the dog is always at his side, so petting it is something to ask for: the 🐾 button, P, or "🐾 Pet the dog" in the dog's own panel. It comes and sits in front of him, facing him (never beside him or with its back to him); he stands before it, just clear of its front paws and chest (worked out from its sitting shape), leans over a little and strokes the top of its head from the front while it wags hard and nuzzles up into his hand; afterwards it gets up and follows him again. Walking off calls it off. (Not in the Learning Hall or the Universal Hall: the dog stays out of the two shrine halls and waits by their steps.)

**Small touches:** now and then a frangipani flower (ลีลาวดี) lets go of one of the trees nearby and drifts down, turning, to lie in the grass a while; the Bodhi tree drops its leaves the same way. Calm motion stops new ones falling. Every so often (never with calm motion on) a gentle shower passes over the temple for a minute or two: soft rain (none under the roofs), the light dims a little, rings spread on the moat and the ponds, the pigeons wait it out in the trees, and with the sound on there is the hush of rain, muffled inside the halls.

**Shoes off at every hall:** as at a Thai temple, shoes stay outside. Walking up to any hall (or the open Rest pavilion), the boy stops on the step, looks down and slips out of his sneakers one foot at a time; the pair is left neatly on the step beside him, toes turned out, with his school backpack standing beside it (sitting and bowing with a bag on isn't done either), and he goes in in his white socks. Coming out, he steps back into his shoes and takes his bag. Inside he is a second model of the same boy, in socks and without the backpack (made from the same artwork, with the same skeleton and the same idle, walk, run and sitting animations), so the wai, sitting, the kraap and everything posed on his skeleton work the same; the measurements those poses rely on (his hands, his fingers' direction, his forehead) are taken from whichever model he is wearing. Arriving inside some other way (the places menu, coming back from a page) he is simply in socks with his shoes at the step. Only the Sambodhaya boy changes; the built characters have no shoes to take off. The socks model loads after the boy appears, so the first steps are never delayed.

**Giving alms (ตักบาตร):** monks eat only between dawn and midday, so the alms round is a morning sight: from 6:00 until 11:00 by the visitor's own clock four monks stand in a row on the entrance road just outside the temple gate, most senior first (the elder, the middle-aged monk, the young monk with glasses and a novice), each holding his black alms bowl open. On an alms round the robe covers both shoulders (ห่มคลุม), so these monks are dressed that way. A low table beside the road (off it, clear of the way) holds a silver bowl of sticky-rice balls. Standing in front of a monk and pressing E (🍚 ใส่บาตร), the boy steps up and faces him, holds a rice ball in both hands, raises it to his forehead, places it in the bowl (his eyes on the bowl, the monk leaning in slightly to receive), then kneels with his hands in a wai at the chest, head a little bowed and eyes closed, while the monk gives the blessing (below), and stands again. His hips and shoulders turn square to the monk for the offering, and the rice is held between his palms (placed by measurement of his hands). The rice stays in the bowl for that morning; nothing is counted. In the afternoon, evening and night the road is empty and the rice table is gone (an offering in progress is never cut short). In Thailand givers usually take off their shoes; the boy's shoes are part of his model, so he keeps them on.

**Monks on the platform:** three different monks (an elder, a middle-aged monk and a young monk with glasses), in the same animated-film style as the schoolboy, sit cross-legged on the teachers' seats in the Learning Hall, the temple's main hall (the Universal Hall's seats stand empty). Their robes are the muted brown of the forest tradition (not bright orange), worn temple-style with the right shoulder bare; they always sit lower than the Buddha image. They breathe slowly (still when calm motion is on) and are scenery only: they do not speak or teach, so the 3D world still holds no teaching content of its own.

**The Sambodhaya girl:** under 🧍 My avatar, children can choose the Sambodhaya girl, the boy's classmate, instead of the boy. She is in the same animated-film style, in a Thai school uniform fit for a temple: a white blouse whose sleeves cover the shoulders and upper arms, with a navy bow, and a navy pleated skirt to mid-calf, well below the knee; white socks, black school shoes and a black school backpack. She is rigged on the same skeleton as the boy with her own walk, idle and sitting animations (she doesn't run: a running stride would kick her legs out through her long skirt, and one walks in a temple anyway, so Shift is a brisk walk for her), so everything he does she does the same way (the wai at every level, sitting on a cushion, the kraap, giving alms, the Kathina, the kneeling offerings, feeding the fish, sweeping, petting the dog and the cat). At the hall steps she slips out of her school shoes and leaves them with her bag, and goes in in her socks (a second model of her, in socks and without the backpack), just as he does. When she sits (on a cushion in a hall, or anywhere) she sits พับเพียบ, as is polite before the monks and the Buddha image: her pelvis rests on the cushion, settled onto her left hip, back upright, both knees pointing forward and both lower legs folded to her right with the feet behind, her hands resting one on the other on her thighs and her eyes lowered; her long skirt stays smooth over her knees. (No animation for this exists, so it is posed on her skeleton, like the kraap.) The choice is remembered on the device.

**The Sambodhaya boy (default avatar):** the boy from the Sambodhaya film as a rigged 3D character (Higgsfield: a clean picture made from the film's frames, then Meshy image-to-3D with a 24-bone skeleton). He has idle, casual-walk and run animations (`boy.glb` holds the model and walk; `boy-idle.glb` and `boy-run.glb` hold only an animation for the same skeleton). Each clip's stride is measured when it loads and the clip plays at the speed that keeps his feet planted; footstep sounds follow his steps. Normal movement is a calm walk, Shift (or the touch stick pushed to its edge) runs. The block builder, picture-face and 3D-file avatars remain under "My avatar". **The wai (🙏 button, Q, or the 🙏 touch button):** palms pressed flat together in front of the chest, fingers together and upright, fingertips meeting at the chin, head facing forward with a modest bow; it holds briefly and rises again. The height follows Thai custom and where he stands: in front of a Buddha image (the Learning Hall and Universal Hall shrines) he turns to face it and makes the highest wai - thumbs between the eyebrows, fingertips at the hairline, a deeper bow and a longer hold; near a monk or a novice (the monk at walking meditation, the novice sweeping, the monks on the alms round) he turns to the nearest of them and makes the same highest wai, as monks always receive; in the caretaker's kuti, the wai to elders - fingertips between the eyebrows; elsewhere, the everyday wai - fingertips at the nose. The heights are points on his own face, measured from his head mesh, and the hands follow the face as he bows. The hands are placed from the boy's real hand mesh (the palm side is the hand's thinnest direction that faces the body at rest), so the fingertips meet exactly; where the thick cartoon palms would pass the middle they are pressed flat against each other in the shader, as real palms flatten, so the hands never pass through each other. No ready-made wai animation exists, so it is posed on the boy's own skeleton (a two-bone reach solver places the arms; the bow is spread over the spine, neck and head) on top of whatever he is doing; walking away ends it gently. The block avatar does a simpler block-style wai.

**Sitting (🧘 button, C, or the 🧘 touch button):** the boy lowers himself into a cross-legged sit (ขัดสมาธิ, a ready-made floor-sitting animation) with his eyes lowered and his hands resting in his lap, palms up, the right hand on the left (posed on his skeleton; the lap height is measured from his seated legs, and the two thick hands are pressed flat where they meet). Near a cushion in a hall he settles onto it (his seat on the cushion and his crossed legs on the floor around it, measured from his seated pose), facing the front - the altar and the teachers' platform - to meditate or listen; before a Buddha image he faces the image. A wai while seated works (in a shrine hall, the high wai). The temple dog comes and sits beside him (its body tips back over the front paws so the haunches rest on the ground, in its vertex shader) and gets up when he does. Walking, or the button again, stands him up. The block avatar sits with legs forward and hands on its knees.

**Paying tribute to the Buddha (🙇 กราบพระ button or K):** in front of the Buddha image in the Learning Hall or the Universal Hall, the boy walks to the aisle before the altar (far enough back that his bowed head stays clear of the platform), faces the image and kneels (toes tucked, as boys kneel) with his hands in a wai at the chest. He raises a high wai (fingertips at the hairline), then bows three times (กราบ 3 ครั้ง): forehead to the floor with both palms flat on the floor beside it, then back up to kneeling with the high wai each time. After the third bow he lowers the wai to his chest and stands. It takes about 14 seconds and is posed on his skeleton (the forehead and palms are placed on the floor by measurement, the knees stay on the floor). The temple dog sits quietly while he bows (and likewise through the Kathina offering and while he gives alms, including the wai that follows). The dog never enters the Learning Hall, the main hall where the monks sit: when the boy goes in, it waits outside beside the steps, sitting and facing the door, and follows him again when he comes out. If he stays inside a while (by day), it trots off to keep the sweeping novice company, walking at his side and sitting when he stops, and comes running back when the boy comes out. Sweeping, the novice's broom (and the child's own) makes a soft swish on the paving. Elsewhere, the button explains where it can be done.

**Movement:** the temple dog trots (diagonal legs swing together, paws lift, head bobs) and wags its tail; the model has no skeleton, so the walk is done in its vertex shader, shadow included. The Practice Garden waterfall runs: streaks flow down a curved sheet of water in two layers, foam turns where it lands, ripples spread across the pool, droplets splash and a soft mist hangs. Calm motion slows the water and the tail rather than stopping them.

**Donations in 3D (for adults):** a red-and-gold donation box (ตู้รับบริจาค) on the front of the inner gate's "leave any time" post; standing at it and pressing E opens a short note (for adults, entirely optional, the temple is the same for everyone) and a link to the support page. Quiet "(for adults)" links also sit in the ❔ help and 🗺️ places panels. All of them carry the way back to the 3D temple.

**Between the 3D temple and the site:** pages opened from inside the 3D temple (a hall, a story board, 💬 feedback) carry `?from=3d`; there the site's "back to courtyard" becomes "↩️🏯 back to the 3D temple", and you return to the exact spot, facing and view you left (kept for this browser tab only). The browser's back button returns there too. Pages opened directly on the site, and the 3D temple's "leave" and "no 3D map" links, behave as before.

**The naga bridge (ราวนาค):** the bridge over the moat has naga railings, as at Thai temples: each handrail is the serpent's golden scaled body (gold scales with green and red glass inlay, a crest of gold fins along its back), gently undulating on white balusters, and at each of the bridge's four ends it rises from a white pedestal into a rearing many-headed naga that looks out along the road.

**The great white Buddha:** above the waterfall at the back of the Practice Garden, a great white marble Buddha image sits on a rock terrace, facing the temple, like the great white Buddhas on Thai hillsides: in the Maravijaya posture (ปางมารวิชัย, the right hand touching the earth), with the flame finial on the head, on a double lotus seat, about 21 m tall at this scale, rising above the halls so it can be seen across the grounds. The water seems to pour from beneath its seat. At night two soft floodlights light it (on the full-quality setting). The Practice Garden's sign hangs beside the waterfall so it hides neither the fall nor the image.

**The guardian giants (ยักษ์ทวารบาล):** as at the gates of Wat Arun, two yaksha guard the gateway in the temple wall, on white pedestals either side of it just outside the wall, facing out over the naga bridge to meet those who arrive: a green one on the left and a white one on the right, with tiered golden crowns, ornate costumes and their clubs (กระบอง). They are made in the same animated-film style as the boy, friendly and protective rather than fearsome.

**Fish in the moat, fed from the bridge:** koi and golden carp (36, or 16 in light mode) swim the moat, each patrolling its own stretch, with more of them near the bridge; the moat's water is clearer than the ponds' so they show beneath it. On the bridge, pressing E (🐟 ให้อาหารปลา) sends the boy to the rail on his side, where he tosses three handfuls of fish food; the pellets arc down and ripple where they land, and the fish nearby hurry over, crowd at the surface and nibble, then drift back to their stretch. The dog sits beside him meanwhile.

**The monks' meal (🍚 ถวายภัตตาหาร):** monks eat only between dawn and noon, so in the main hall, by the visitor's own clock, the three teachers have their morning meal (7:00–9:00) and the late-morning meal, ฉันเพล (11:00–12:00), where they sit. During a meal, a tray of food for each monk (rice, two dishes, soup and a glass of water) waits on a low table on the floor before the platform. Before each monk the child can offer his tray (🍚 ถวายภัตตาหาร, from the list before him) (ประเคน): kneeling, lifting it with both hands, and the monk receiving it with both hands; the girl sets it instead on the cloth he lays out to receive it (ผ้ารับประเคน), as women do not hand things to a monk directly. The tray then goes before him. When all three are offered, the child stays kneeling with a wai while the senior monk gives the blessing (the Abhivādana verse); then the monks eat, quietly and slowly, each holding his plate of rice in the left hand and eating with a spoon in the right, until the meal time ends. A quest, *The monks' meal*, follows these steps (the quest list says when the meals are). To preview it at any hour, open `world.html?meal` (and `world.html?alms` for the alms round).

**Greeting the monks (🙏 ไหว้พระสงฆ์):** anywhere in the area before each of the three teachers in the main hall, the prompt 🙏 พระสงฆ์ (A monk) lists what can be done there: greet him, and at meal times offer his meal, and before the senior monk offer the Kathina robe. With one thing to do, E does it; with more, E opens a short list to choose from, and the child moves to the right place to kneel by himself. Choosing to greet him (🙏 Greet the monk), the child kneels with a wai, as a child always does before a seated monk, looking at him. The monk looks up at the child and says a few words, a different line each visit in turn: a greeting or a short teaching for children (four lines each, checked by a former monk in Thailand; for example *"A calm mind is like still water: in it you can see everything clearly"*). Each has his own recorded voice (MiniMax: the senior monk Alistair, the monk of about forty Harrison, the young monk Brooks; `audio/talk-<senior|middle|junior>-<1–4>-<th|en>.mp3`), heard from where he sits with a light echo of the hall (only with the sound on), and his words are shown beneath (always). Then the child bows the head a little and stands. Greeting a monk also ticks that step of the first-visit quest.

**The story paintings:** each of the eight story boards in the Learning Hall (U01–U08) carries a painting in the manner of Thai temple murals (flat, gold on deep red), showing its story only in symbols taken from its source record, with no figures: a folded robe and alms bowl on a path out to the forest (going forth, MN26); two bundles of palm-leaf texts and the path going on (Āḷāra and Uddaka); a bare tree with a small lamp still burning (the harsh practice, history only); a rose-apple tree and a bowl of rice (MN36); the wheel of Dhamma with two deer at Isipatana (SN56.11); an eight-spoked wheel above a lotus (SN45.8); a lamp beside the texts of the Dhamma and the Vinaya (DN16); and twin sal trees in bloom (DN16). The story's dialog shows the painting larger, with a description for screen readers (`img/murals/u01–u08.jpg`).

**Cards: what things are, and why (ℹ️):** clicking or tapping a thing in the temple shows a short card in Thai or English: the Bodhi tree, the guardian giants, the naga, the bell, the lotus, the great white Buddha, a hall's main Buddha image, the stepped altar, the phan phum, the Kathina gifts, the triangle cushions, the holy water and its whisk, and (on the morning alms round) the alms bowl. Outdoors, walking up to the Bodhi tree, a guardian giant or the lotus jars shows ℹ️ with the card's name, so E opens it too. Clicking or tapping one of the seated monks in the main hall opens what can be done before him (as walking up to him and pressing E does).

**Morning mist:** by the visitor's clock, a soft mist lies over the temple at dawn: it rises from 5:00, is thickest from about 5:45 to 7:15 and has lifted by 8:45. The distance fades into a pale haze and low banks of mist drift slowly over the lawns and the moat, never through the halls. `world.html?mist` shows it at any hour.

**Festivals:** the great days follow the Thai lunar calendar, each on a full moon. The full moons are worked out in the page (Meeus' method, to within about an hour), and each festival is the first full moon on or after a day of the year: Makha Bucha from 7 February, Visakha Bucha from 9 May, Asalha Bucha from 8 July, Loy Krathong from 30 October (these give the actual dates of 2020 and 2023–2026, leap-month years included). Its decorations stay from the day before that full moon until the day after, by Thai time. On **Loy Krathong**, red Lanna paper lanterns hang on strings across the entrance road, glowing warmer at night, and krathongs of banana leaf and lotus petals with a lit candle and incense drift slowly round the moat; on the bridge the child can float one (🪷 ลอยกระทง, from a short list with feeding the fish and a card on what Loy Krathong is): kneeling at the edge, raising it to the forehead for a wish, setting it down, and it glides down onto the water and drifts away with the others. On the **Bucha days** (Makha, Visakha and Asalha Bucha) candles on low stands line the walk all round the main hall for the candle procession (เวียนเทียน), with a card on what it is. `world.html?loykrathong` or `world.html?bucha` shows either at any time.

**The meditation corner (🧘 ฝึกนั่งสมาธิ):** sitting on a cushion in a hall, or anywhere in the Practice Garden, a small panel offers a guided sitting of about one minute or three. A calm voice (MiniMax, Mark; `audio/medi-<1–8>-<th|en>.mp3`) says a few lines with long quiet between them (sit comfortably; close the eyes or look down; don't control the breath; breathing in, know you are breathing in; if the mind wanders, come back; and, in the longer one, the air at the tip of the nose and "a calm mind is like still water"), each shown beneath as it is said. The breath is never held or counted, only known as it is. A small singing bowl rings at the start and the end, the sounds of the world outside grow quieter and a warm glow gathers at the edges of the screen. Standing up, walking or Stop ends it at any time; nothing is scored. The voice and the bowl are heard only with the sound on.

**The top buttons:** only the three main ones stay in view (🗺️ places, 📜 quests, 📖 passport) with ☰ for the rest (avatar, picture quality, wai, sit, tribute, pet, sound, night, calm motion, feedback, help, leave). The menu folds again after a choice, or a tap on the temple; switches such as sound and night leave it open. Whether it was left open is remembered on this device.

**Photo mode (📷 ถ่ายภาพ, in the ☰ menu):** every button, note and panel steps aside but a small bar (＋ － to zoom, 📸 to take the photo, ✕ when done); the camera can also come closer and go further than usual, and on a phone the walking stick stays. A photo is the picture as it is, with "☸ วัดสัมโพธายะ · Sambodhaya" in the corner; it opens with 💾 Save and, where the phone allows, 📤 Share. Nothing is uploaded: the photo stays on the device unless the child saves or shares it.

**Two players on one device (👫 เล่นสองคน, in the ☰ menu):** a second child joins (the girl if player 1 is the boy, or another of the same), standing beside player 1. Each has a coloured ring at their feet: player 1 orange, player 2 blue. On a computer player 1 has W A S D (and E, Q, C, K, P, left Shift, Space), player 2 the arrow keys with Enter to act, . to wai, , to sit, / to jump and right Shift to walk briskly; on a tablet or phone each has a stick in their own bottom corner with small buttons above it (act, wai, sit, jump). One view keeps both in frame, pulling back as they walk apart; they can wander up to about 15 m apart and don't walk through each other. Each child does their own things: walking, the wai, sitting (not on the same cushion), shoes off and on at the hall steps, feeding the fish, petting the dog or the cat (one child at a time per animal), sweeping, watering, folding a lotus, meditating, cards, the bell, and each gets their own note of what they can do there, on their own side of the screen. The temple's one-of-a-kind things belong to whoever is using them: one broom, one watering can, one folding mat. **Ceremonies are done together** (as a former monk in Thailand described them): when one child begins a kneeling ceremony (paying respect to the Buddha, the Kathina, an offering of incense, candles and a lotus or of gold leaf, the monks' meal, greeting a teacher), the other comes and kneels at their left, a little behind, and follows the same timeline, with the wai and the bows together; while the giver hands something over, the other child's right hand rests on the giver's arm, sharing in the merit (boys and girls may touch each other's arm in a ceremony). At the alms round each child puts rice in the bowl of a monk of their own, side by side, and they kneel together for the one blessing. A child who is busy with something of their own (feeding the fish, their shoes at the steps, the can or the folding mat), or far away, doesn't join. One passport and one set of quests for the device. The menu choice again ends it.

**The temple passport (📖 สมุดตรา):** a little book of round stamps, one for each thing found or done in the temple: the places (the courtyard, each hall, the Practice Garden, My Journey, the Bodhi tree, the great Buddha image, the naga bridge, and striking the bell), the customs (shoes off at the hall, three bows, greeting a monk, sitting quietly in a hall, a meditation to its end, incense, candles and a lotus, gold leaf, alms, the meal, the Kathina, and on Loy Krathong floating a krathong), friends (the dog, the cat, the fish and each of the three teachers greeted) and one for each card read (ℹ️). A stamp is earned by doing the thing, wherever it is done, quest or not, and a small note says so. Stamps not yet found are faint, with their names, so a child knows what there is still to find; festival-only stamps appear only on those days, or once earned. There is no score, and the stamps are kept on this device only.

**Quests (📜 ภารกิจ / Quests button):** six quests (the sixth, *The monks' meal*, is described above), each one custom done as at a real temple, as a short checklist that ticks itself when the child does each thing, with one line on why it is done (in Thai or English): *A first visit to the temple* (take off your shoes at a hall, bow three times before the Buddha, greet a monk with a wai, sit quietly on a cushion), *Giving alms in the morning* (put rice in a monk's bowl, kneel with a wai for the blessing; the alms round is only from 6:00 to 11:00, which the quest list says), *Making merit in the halls* (incense, candles and a lotus; gold leaf), *The Kathina* (offer the robe; bow three times and receive the holy water and the blessing) and *Friends in the temple* (feed the fish, stroke the dog, stroke the cat). Steps may be done in any order; there are no scores and no failing. One quest is followed at a time, its checklist in a small box (tap its title to fold it), and a quest done is marked ✓ in the list. Progress is kept on this device only.

**Walking low before the monks, and gentle hints:** when the child passes close in front of the seated monks in the main hall, the child walks with the body lowered and the head bowed (เดินก้ม), and a little more slowly. Small notes explain a custom once per visit, when it comes up: why one walks low before the monks; to walk calmly in a hall and near the monks (when running there); after bowing to the Buddha, to step back a few paces before turning away (when the child turns straight away); and, for the girl giving alms or the Kathina robe, that women do not touch a monk or hand things to him directly, which is why the rice goes into the bowl and the robe is set down for him.

**The monks' blessing (อนุโมทนา):** after the Kathina (while the senior monk sprinkles the holy water) and after giving alms (while the child kneels with a wai), the monk recites the Abhivādana verse, the closing verse of the anumodana (Dhammapada 109): อะภิวาทะนะสีลิสสะ นิจจัง วุฑฒาปะจายิโน จัตตาโร ธัมมา วัฑฒันติ อายุ วัณโณ สุขัง พะลัง, "for one who always honours and respects the elders, four things grow: long life, beauty, happiness and strength". It is a recorded recitation (MiniMax, voice Alistair, read the Thai way; `audio/bless-hall.mp3` with a soft hall echo in the main hall, `audio/bless.mp3` outdoors), heard from where the monk is, and the verse and its meaning are shown beneath while it plays (in Thai or English). The voice is heard only with the sound (🔈) on; the words are shown either way. (Sounds made inside a hall, like the holy water, are no longer muffled as if they came from outside.)

**Blinking and closed eyes:** every character blinks: the boy and the girl, the monks (the teachers in the main hall, the seated monks, the walking monk, the novice and the monks on the alms round), the dog and the cat. Each blinks in its own time, every 2 to 6½ seconds, in a fifth of a second. The models have no eyelids of their own (the eyes are painted on), so the lids are drawn on the graphics card: each eye is an ellipsoid measured on that model, and as the lid closes it is painted the colour of the skin round that eye, matte and without the eye's painted relief, with a dark line of lashes along the edge. Eyes also stay closed when it fits: the child's while bowing with the hands in a wai (the kraap, the Kathina, the kneeling offerings, the blessing after giving alms) and while sitting on a cushion; the senior monk's while he blesses with holy water; the dog's while it lies down to be stroked. The sleeping cat lies curled with her face tucked in, and the stretching cat's eyes are shut in the model itself.

**Day and night (🌙 / ☀️ button):** at night the sunset sky gives way to a deep blue sky with stars and a moon, and soft moonlight. Every lamp post is lit, with a warm glow at its lantern and a pool of light on the ground. The halls are lit from inside: the hall lights burn brighter and the hanging lanterns glow, as do the candles at the shrines. The floating hall signs are softened so they read without glare. Real lights are costly, so on the full-quality setting three of them follow the visitor to the nearest lamp posts and light whatever is near them; the other lamps glow without casting light. It follows the clock of the visitor's own device: day from 6:00 to 18:00 and night otherwise. Day does not switch to night at once: the change takes the hour around 18:00 (and around 6:00 for dawn), through a golden dusk or dawn: the light warms and lowers, a gold and rose glow lies along the horizon, the stars and moon come out, the halls light up from inside, and the lamp posts come on one by one, from the middle of the temple out to the gate and down the road. The 🌙 / ☀️ button switches by hand for that visit (an eight-second dusk or dawn), until the clock's next change or a reload. At night the novice has gone in and the pigeons roost.

**The gate and the bell:** the gate's two wooden doors are each half the gateway wide and hinged on its posts, so closed they would shut it completely; they stand open, swung in along the sides of the road, and are solid to walk into. The two lamp posts nearest the gate stand just outside it, in front of the gate posts, so nothing is in the doors' way. The temple bell stands beside the entrance road and can be rung from the road.

**Sound (`src/sound.js`):** made in the browser with Web Audio, nothing is downloaded. Following the charter, nothing plays until the visitor presses something: the 🔈 button (off every time the page opens) starts the ambience, and the temple bell rings only when struck (walk to it, press E). The site-wide "turn off all sound" setting is respected: the button and the bell ask first. Sounds are placed in 3D:
- breeze and rustling leaves that follow slow gusts, and small roof bells (kradueng) at the hall eaves that tinkle in the gusts
- birds in the trees: bulbul-like twitters and, now and then, the Asian koel's rising "ko-el"
- the Practice Garden waterfall
- footsteps: stone on paths and the plaza, soft on grass, a quiet pad of socks on the marble inside halls
- inside a hall the outside world is muffled and quieter
- the bronze temple bell: a deep strike with a long, beating ring
- pigeons clattering up when they take off
- the temple cat purring while she is petted
- a passing shower: the hush of rain, muffled inside the halls
- the time of day: birds by day; at night crickets, frogs calling by the ponds and the moat (the bullfrog's deep "oong-aang" and small frogs' rattles), and now and then the tokay gecko's "tuk-kae" from a hall's eaves
- the temple gong at dawn and dusk: three slow strokes from the Learning Hall when day turns to night or night to day (also when the 🌙 / ☀️ button is pressed), only while the ambience is on
- water lapping at the banks, louder the closer he is to the moat, coming from the point of the moat nearest him

## Form submissions in Supabase

Every accepted Netlify Forms submission (spam filtered out) is also copied into Supabase by `netlify/functions/submission-created.mjs`, which Netlify runs on its own. The submission always stays in Netlify too; if Supabase is not set up or refuses a row, nothing is lost.

The rows go to the **Goosemania Kanban board** Supabase project (no extra cost), in two tables of their own: **sambodhaya_donations** (submitted, name, amount_thb, method, note, email, thanks, language, slip, submission_id, delete_after, status: Awaiting check / Verified / Problem) and **sambodhaya_feedback** (submitted, type, message, page, language, device, email, attachment, submission_id). View and edit them in the Supabase dashboard → Table Editor.

The tables are closed to the API. The site's only way in is the database function `sambodhaya_submit(form, fields, secret)`, which adds one row and refuses calls without the secret kept in `private.sambodhaya_settings`. So the site cannot read anything, in these tables or in the Kanban board.

Settings, in Netlify → Site configuration → Environment variables (never in the code or in chat): `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_KEY` (the project's publishable key) and `SAMBODHAYA_SECRET` (the value in `private.sambodhaya_settings`).

The support page promises slips are kept 90 days: each donation row gets a `delete_after` date, and a nightly database job (pg_cron, `sambodhaya-delete-old-donations`, 03:15 UTC) removes the row on that date. Delete the submission in Netlify (Forms → donation) by then too. The slip link is Netlify's; if it has expired, open the submission in the Netlify dashboard by its submission_id.
